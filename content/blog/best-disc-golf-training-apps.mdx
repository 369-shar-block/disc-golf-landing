<Answer>

The right disc golf app depends on the job. For scorekeeping and course maps, UDisc is built for that and is free to start. For measuring the disc itself, TechDisc (a $299.99 sensor disc) or RipReader (a phone camera app) list speed and spin. For seeing what your body does during the throw, video form apps such as Disc-i, Snapdisc or our own Disc Golf Form Analyzer are the category to look at.

## Form analysis apps

These apps look at your body, usually from a video you film on your phone. If you have never filmed yourself, start with [how to film a disc golf throw for form analysis](/blog/how-to-film-disc-golf-throw-for-form-analysis), because every app in this group depends on a clean clip. For how the software side works and where it falls short, see [how AI disc golf form analysis works](/blog/how-ai-disc-golf-form-analysis-works).

### Disc-i (formerly Disc.ai)

Disc-i runs pose analysis on your video and compares it with ranges taken from pro throwers. It covers backhand, forehand and putts, and its listing says it scores arm path, brace, coil and posture. Paid pro overlays let you put your throw next to a pro's. It is the closest app to ours in what it does. If comparing yourself with pros matters to you, or you want pose-based putting feedback, Disc-i lists both and we do not grade putts by pose. Price: $6.99 a month or $69.99 a year, on iOS and Android. Source: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/disc-i/id6447535006) and [disciapp.com](https://www.disciapp.com/), checked October 2026.

### ScoreSensei

ScoreSensei combines skeletal tracking against elite form with voice scoring and round coaching. That makes it the one app here whose listing covers both form work and scorekeeping, which suits players who would rather keep one app on the phone. The App Store lists in-app purchases from $3.99 to $49.99, but we could not confirm the subscription periods, so check the listing. Its website mentions Android; we could not verify that, so we list iOS only. Source: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/scoresensei-disc-golf/id6758869856), checked October 2026.

### Snapdisc

Snapdisc focuses on video review basics: slow motion, side-by-side playback with pro throws, and a pose angle overlay. If you mainly want to scrub through your throw next to a reference and judge it yourself, it does that, with listed in-app prices ($1.99 to $8.99). It runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Source: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/snapdisc-disc-golf-form-app/id6471839863), checked October 2026.

### Formero

Formero takes a different route: human coaches review the videos you send. If you want a person, not software, to look at your form, this is the type of app to consider. The listing we found did not publish a price. iOS. Source: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/formero/id6470127092), checked October 2026.

### OnForm

OnForm is a general sports video analysis tool, not made for disc golf. It gives you manual tools for drawing, comparing and annotating video, which suits coaches or players who know what they are looking for. It runs on iOS, Android and the web. We could not verify its current price, so see its pricing page. Source: [onform.com/pricing](https://onform.com/pricing/), checked October 2026.

### Disc Golf Form Analyzer (ours)

DGFA grades backhand and forehand video against a real coach's ideal form, on 20 coach-defined faults (10 per throw type), and it adjusts for the camera angle you filmed from. Each fault comes with the coach's cue, a drill and a demo clip. A separate AI Analysis gives a written breakdown (what is working, the main issue, root cause, fix, drill) for backhand, forehand and putts, and you can ask follow-ups in DGFA Coach chat. 3D Throw rebuilds your throw as a 3D body you can view from any angle at quarter speed, or compare two throws synced at release (AR on iPhone only, up to 2 new 3D builds per day). DGFA Caddie stores your bag and suggests discs hole by hole. It does not keep score, show course maps, or measure disc speed, spin or distance. $39.99 a year after a 3-day free trial, iPhone and Android. Source: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/disc-golf-form-analyzer/id6755727208), checked October 2026.

## Throw measurement apps

These measure what the disc does, not what your body does. Numbers like speed and spin tell you the result of a throw; a form app tries to explain the cause. Many players use both.

### TechDisc

TechDisc is a sensor built into a disc. It lists speed, spin, nose angle, launch angle, hyzer and wobble, plus a simulator. If you want hard numbers on disc flight, this is the category to choose, and DGFA does not measure any of these. The disc costs $299.99. The app is free, with paid tiers at $4.99 and $9.99 a month. iOS, Android and web. Source: [features and pricing](https://shop.techdisc.com/pages/features-pricing) and [shop](https://shop.techdisc.com/collections/all), checked October 2026.

### RipReader

RipReader uses your phone camera as a "radar gun" for disc speed, spin and distance, so no extra hardware is needed. Pro is $24.99 a year; Premium is $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year. iOS and Mac. Source: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ripreader-disc-golf-radar-gun/id6760834017), checked October 2026.

## How to choose by goal

Pick the job first, then the app. Many players end up with two: one for rounds, one for practice.

- **Track rounds, keep score, find courses.** UDisc is built for this. Upsi and Disc Golf Metrix are free alternatives, Metrix especially for leagues and events. DGFA does none of this.
- **Measure the disc.** TechDisc if you want sensor data and the $299.99 disc fits your budget. RipReader if you want camera-based speed, spin and distance with no extra hardware. DGFA does not measure the disc.
- **Fix your form.** A video form app. Disc-i compares you with pro ranges and covers putts. Snapdisc keeps it to slow motion and side-by-side. Formero puts a human coach on your clip. DGFA grades you against one coach's ideal form and gives a fix and drill per fault ([how Pose Estimation works](/features/pose-estimation)). For a worked example of one common fault, see [how to stop rounding your backhand](/blog/how-to-stop-rounding-disc-golf-backhand).
- **Learn on video yourself.** If you want to study your own clips and draw your own conclusions, OnForm and Snapdisc give you manual tools and slow motion. DGFA's [3D Throw](/features/3d-throw) lets you spin your throw to angles a single camera misses.
- **Plan shots and pick discs.** Radius for maps and AI disc picks; DiscScout for GPS throw tracking; [DGFA Caddie](/features/caddie) if you want picks from the discs in your own bag.
