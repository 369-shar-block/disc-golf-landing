A useful disc golf form check starts with good clips: side-on and from behind, in landscape, whole body in frame, in good light. Add the context people need (the disc, the distance, the flight, your throwing hand and the shot you wanted) and ask one specific question. Then judge the replies: trust advice that points to a moment in your clip and gives one thing to try. You can also run your own form check with the checklist below.

Form checks are everywhere. In our own count of r/discgolf over 12 months, 510 posts carried the "Form Check" flair, on top of a weekly form check thread, and [r/DiscGolfForm](https://www.reddit.com/r/DiscGolfForm/) saw 932 posts in the same period. The ones that get useful replies have something in common: you can actually see the throw, and the poster gives people enough to go on. The ones that get "can't tell, film from another angle" are usually missing one of a handful of basics.

Full disclosure: I build Disc Golf Form Analyzer, an app that grades filmed throws. This guide is mostly about getting good feedback from people, and about checking your own form.

## Step 1: Film something people can read

You cannot get a good form check from a bad clip. These are the essentials; the full detail is in [how to film your disc golf throw for form analysis](/blog/how-to-film-disc-golf-throw-for-form-analysis).

1. **Two angles.** Side-on (camera square to your chest at the plant) and directly behind, looking down the target line.
2. **Landscape.** A throw moves sideways. Portrait clips cut off the run-up and follow-through.
3. **Whole body, the whole time.** Head to plant foot, from the start of the run-up to the end of the follow-through.
4. **Phone at about hip height, steady,** roughly 15 to 25 feet away.
5. **Good light, sun behind the camera.** A backlit thrower is a silhouette.
6. **Slow motion if you have it,** but post a full-speed version too. Slow motion hides timing problems.
7. **A few throws.** One representative throw is fine for a post, but film several so you can pick a typical one, not your best or worst.

### Which angle shows what

| Want feedback on | Film from |
|---|---|
| Plant timing, weight shift, posture, brace | Side-on |
| Rounding, power pocket, early release | Behind |
| Opening up early, forehand elbow (chicken wing) | Front |
| Everything at once | Side-on plus behind |

Nose angle is the one thing no phone angle shows well. If you are asking about nose-up releases, say how the disc flies, because that is what people will judge it from.

## Step 2: Include the context

The same throw can be fine or broken depending on what you meant to do. Give people:

- **Throwing hand and throw type.** "Right-hand backhand" or "left-hand forehand."
- **The disc.** Mold and, if you know them, the flight numbers. A very understable disc turning over is different from a stable disc turning over.
- **Distance and flight.** How far it went and what it did: "flies about 260 ft, turns right early and never fades."
- **The shot you wanted.** Flat drive, hyzer, anhyzer, max distance, controlled upshot.
- **Standstill or run-up.** And roughly how long you have played, if it is relevant.
- **Pain, if any.** If something hurts, say so. People will steer you differently, and you should see a medical professional for pain rather than rely on a form check.

## Step 3: Ask one specific question

"Thoughts?" gets you twelve unrelated opinions. A specific question gets focused answers.

Weak questions:

- "What am I doing wrong?"
- "How do I throw farther?"
- "Rate my form."

Better questions:

- "My drives start right of my line. Am I rounding, or releasing early?"
- "My front leg feels like it collapses. Does it look that way on the side clip?"
- "I lose about 40 ft when I add an x-step versus a standstill. What changes in my plant?"

Notice the pattern: a symptom you see in the flight, plus your guess at the cause. Even if your guess is wrong, it gives people something to confirm or correct.

## Step 4: Judge the advice you get

Most form check replies are well meant. Not all are right, and they often conflict. Here is how to sort them.

### Signs of good advice

- **It points to a moment.** "At about the plant, your chest is already facing the target." You can go and check that frame.
- **It explains why.** "So the hips have nothing left to turn, and the arm does all the work."
- **It gives one thing to try.** A drill, a cue or a position.
- **Others agree.** When several people independently name the same issue, it is probably real.
- **It matches what you see.** When you go to the frame they mentioned, you can see it too.

### Signs to be cautious

- **A list of eight faults.** Some may be real, but nobody can fix eight things at once. Ask which one matters most.
- **Generic advice.** "Relax," "throw harder" or "just do field work" without anything tied to your clip.
- **"Copy this pro."** Watching good players helps, but their body, flexibility and strength may differ a lot from yours. Copy the order of movement, not exact positions.
- **Advice the clip cannot support.** If someone is confident about your nose angle from a blurry side-on clip, take it lightly.
- **Contradicting the video.** If they say your plant is late but the clip clearly shows your foot down before the arm moves, trust the clip.

### When advice conflicts

It will. One person says you are rounding, another says you are opening up, a third says it is your reach-back. They may all be partly right, because these often go together. To decide:

1. **Check each claim against the clip yourself.** Go to the frame.
2. **Favor the earliest fault.** Throwing is a chain, and the classic biomechanics model describes it as proximal-to-distal: the big segments near the body's center start it, and the arm finishes it ([Putnam 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9290(93)90084-R)). A wide reach-back can cause rounding; fixing the rounding alone treats the symptom.
3. **Try one change for a few sessions.** Then re-film from the same angle.
4. **Compare before and after.** If the position changed and the flight improved, keep going. If not, try the next suggestion.

[Compare disc golf throws side by side](/blog/compare-disc-golf-throws-side-by-side) covers the before-and-after part.

## Step 5: Do your own form check

You do not need to wait for replies. A self form check builds your eye, and it makes the advice you get easier to judge.

Film three or four throws, then step through each one and stop on these moments. Answer each question yes or no.

### Backhand self form check

**Reach-back**
1. Is the disc back along the target line, not swung out to the side?
2. Did your shoulders turn away from the target, not just the arm?
3. Is your head fairly steady, not drifting back?

**Plant**
4. Is your front foot down before the arm starts pulling forward?
5. Is your chest still closed (not facing the target) when the foot lands?

**Pocket**
6. Is the disc close to your chest as it passes?
7. Is your elbow bent and leading toward the target?

**Release**
8. Is your front leg firm, stopping your forward drift?
9. Is your hand close to your body line, not far out to the side?

**Finish**
10. Do you finish balanced over your front foot?

Every "no" is a candidate fault. Pick the earliest one, not the most dramatic.

### Forehand self form check

1. Is your lower body loaded before the arm moves, not just the arm swinging?
2. Is your elbow close to your body, not flaring out (the chicken wing)?
3. Does the movement start from the lower body, not the arm?
4. Does the hand trail the elbow into the hit, rather than pushing the disc forward?
5. Does your weight move onto the front side?
6. Do you finish balanced?

For the forehand basics behind these questions, see [disc golf forehand basics](/blog/disc-golf-forehand-basics).

### What to do with the result

1. Write the main fault in one sentence.
2. Pick one drill for it.
3. Practice in blocks and review in batches, not after every throw. The guidance hypothesis in motor learning suggests that feedback given too often can make a skill harder to keep once the feedback is gone ([Anderson et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1780106/)).
4. Re-film with the same setup and compare.

If rounding is what you found, go to [how to stop rounding your backhand](/blog/how-to-stop-rounding-disc-golf-backhand). For the full process, including every option for getting feedback, see the [disc golf form analysis guide](/blog/disc-golf-form-analysis-guide).

## Disc golf form check checklist

Before you post or review, run through this:

1. Side-on and behind clips, landscape, whole body, good light.
2. Full-speed version included.
3. Throwing hand, disc, distance, flight and intended shot stated.
4. One specific question.
5. Advice checked against the clip, frame by frame.
6. Earliest fault chosen, one drill picked.
7. Re-filmed with the same setup after a few sessions.

Good clips plus a clear question get good answers. And every form check you run yourself makes the next one easier.
